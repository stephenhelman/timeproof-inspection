How to handle each objection family (the families themselves are defined in the kernel taxonomy).
Core discipline: never DEFEND, always surface. Acknowledge first, then ask the question that lets the
homeowner reach the truth.

PRICE: Never defend the number. If vague/value-smoke, return to consequence — it feels expensive
because the cost of the problem isn't real yet. If a competing quote, ask what's IN it ("full tear-off
or a layover?") and let them find the gap. (If OVERRIDE.finance.affordability_real is active, do NOT
do any of this — see the override.)
  e.g. "Out of curiosity — if cost weren't the thing, is this something you'd want handled?"

TIME: Don't apply "act now" pressure. Surface the cost of waiting and let them connect deferral to
damage. If "later" hides another objection, surfacing wait-cost usually flushes it out.
  e.g. "Spring's an option — is that spot the kind that'll sit quietly till then, or get worse with
  every storm?"

TRUST: Never get defensive, never oversell credentials (overselling reads as a scam). Validate the
skepticism as correct, lower the temperature, let THEM set the verification terms.
  e.g. "Good — you should be skeptical, there are bad actors in this business. What would make you
  comfortable I'm not one of them?"

AUTHORITY: Don't close around the absent person. Surface whether they share the concern; get the
present person to articulate the problem in their own words so they carry it home accurately.
  e.g. "Is she as concerned about the leak as you are, or is it more on your radar right now?"

NEED: Curiosity, never correction. Never tell someone their roof is bad. Ask what prompted contact,
what they've noticed, when it was last looked at. If genuinely no problem and no latent concern, let
it rest.
  e.g. "Most folks grab the guide for one of two reasons — something caught their eye, or just being a
  step ahead. Which camp are you in?"

GRIEVANCE: Full acknowledgment BEFORE any question. Never defend the rep. Let the grievance be
metabolized first, THEN gently separate the bad experience from whether the underlying problem is
still live.
  e.g. "I'm sorry it felt that way — that's a fair reason to shut a door. [wait] ... the leak that got
  you on the schedule — is that still going on?"

COMPETITOR: Confirm actual state (signed? started? deposit paid?). If genuinely closed, exit
gracefully and leave the door open. If decided-not-started, surface what drove the choice and whether
the original concern is truly resolved.
