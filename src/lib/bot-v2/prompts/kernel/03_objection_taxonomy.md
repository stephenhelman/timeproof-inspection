The seven objection families. This is the method-neutral map of homeowner resistance — what the
objection IS and whether it's real or a smokescreen. (HOW to handle each is in the methodology layer.)

A SMOKESCREEN is a socially acceptable exit — low detail, portable to any purchase ("need to think
about it", "send me info"). A REAL objection is specific, has emotional weight, and costs something to
say (a specific number, a named constraint, a real grievance). The first objection is rarely the real
one; the real one surfaces only once the homeowner feels no pressure.

1. PRICE / MONEY — "too expensive", "got a cheaper quote", "can't afford it".
   Smoke when vague/no number (means value isn't established). Real when there's a specific competing
   number, or when affordability is genuinely constrained (esp. after a finance decline). Underneath
   smoke: consequence hasn't outweighed cost yet.

2. TIME / URGENCY — "not right now", "maybe spring", "no rush".
   Almost always smoke. A roof problem doesn't pause for a calendar. Underneath: consequence hasn't
   landed, or another objection hides behind "later".

3. TRUST / SKEPTICISM — "how do I know you're legit", "you just knocked on my door".
   Usually REAL and healthy — resistance to being burned, not to fixing the roof. Treating it as
   smoke is the cardinal error.

4. AUTHORITY / DECISION-MAKER — "I need to talk to my spouse".
   Real when there's a genuine absent co-decider; smoke when it's a proxy for the person's own
   hesitation. Tell: spouse invoked BEFORE engaging the problem = often smoke; AFTER genuine
   engagement = real.

5. NEED / PROBLEM DENIAL — "my roof's fine", "just curious".
   Often not an objection at all — pre-problem-awareness. May be genuine (no problem) or latent
   (unvoiced concern). Never correct it; get curious.

6. PRIOR-EXPERIENCE GRIEVANCE — "your rep was pushy", "I felt pressured".
   Real and charged. Must be fully acknowledged before anything else. May itself be the real
   objection, or may mask a competing quote gotten afterward.

7. ALREADY-HANDLED / COMPETITOR — "went with someone else".
   Real if work is signed/done; soft if "decided" but not started (a window). Confirm actual state.
